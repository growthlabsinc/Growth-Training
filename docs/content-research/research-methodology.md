# Research Methodology Documentation

## Executive Summary

This document outlines the research methodology used to collect 62 academic and medical sources for Growth Training educational content. The research focused on accessible, credible medical and scientific information from reputable sources, prioritizing peer-reviewed content where available.

**Research Period**: October 6, 2025
**Total Sources Collected**: 62
**Research Method**: Web-based search of accessible medical literature
**Quality Focus**: Peer-reviewed journals, authoritative medical institutions, recent publications

---

## 1. Research Strategy Overview

### 1.1 Objectives

1. Collect 50+ credible sources across 10 priority topic areas
2. Prioritize peer-reviewed medical and scientific literature
3. Ensure sources are accessible (open access or publicly available)
4. Focus on recent publications (2015-2025) with foundational exceptions
5. Verify source credibility through institutional affiliation and peer-review status

### 1.2 Approach

**Primary Method**: Web search using targeted medical and scientific keywords
**Secondary Validation**: Cross-reference against established medical databases (PubMed, PMC, medical institution websites)
**Quality Control**: Verification of peer-review status, journal impact, institutional reputation

## 4. Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria

### 4.1 Inclusion Criteria

#### Content Requirements
✅ Peer-reviewed publications from recognized journals
✅ Content from authoritative medical institutions (Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, etc.)
✅ Professional medical organization guidelines (AUA, etc.)
✅ Educational content from academic medical centers
✅ Research published 2015-2025 (recent 10 years)
✅ Foundational research (pre-2015) if seminal/highly cited

#### Quality Requirements
✅ Accessible via web (open access or publicly available abstracts)
✅ Authors affiliated with reputable institutions
✅ Clear methodology described (for research studies)
✅ Relevant to PE safety, tissue biology, or male health

### 4.2 Exclusion Criteria

#### Content Exclusions
❌ Blog posts without medical credentials
❌ Reddit posts or forum content
❌ Non-peer-reviewed opinion pieces
❌ Predatory journals (checked against Beall's List concepts)
❌ Studies with significant undisclosed conflicts of interest
❌ Promotional content disguised as research

#### Quality Exclusions
❌ Sources without clear authorship
❌ Content from non-credible institutions
❌ Outdated information contradicted by recent research
❌ Inaccessible sources (hard paywalls with no alternatives)

## 6. Quality Assessment Process

### 6.1 Peer-Review Verification

**Process**:
1. Check journal website for peer-review policy
2. Verify journal appears in recognized databases (PubMed, Scopus)
3. Review editorial board credentials
4. Check journal impact factor where available

**Results**: 47 of 62 sources (76%) are peer-reviewed

### 6.2 Institutional Credibility Check

**Process**:
1. Verify author institutional affiliations
2. Check institution rankings (for academic medical centers)
3. Assess professional organization status (AUA, etc.)
4. Review institutional mission and credibility

**Results**: All sources from credible institutions or recognized authorities

### 6.3 Impact Factor Assessment

**Categories**:
- **Very High**: Nature, PNAS, Science Advances, JCI, Journal of Urology
- **High**: PMC peer-reviewed, Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, StatPearls
- **Medium**: Specialized journals, translational research journals
- **Low**: Educational blogs with medical credentials

**Distribution**: 52% from high or very high impact sources

## 7. Data Extraction Process

### 7.1 Information Collected Per Source

**Bibliographic Data**:
- Authors (primary author or institutional author)
- Publication year
- Article title
- Journal or source name
- Volume/issue (where applicable)
- DOI or PubMed ID (when available)

**Access Information**:
- URL to source
- Open access status (yes/no)

- DOI link (for permanence)

**Categorization**:
- Primary topic category (based on Story 7.1 taxonomy)
- Secondary categories (if applicable)
- Research type (RCT, review, clinical guideline, etc.)
- Relevance score (1-5 scale)

**Quality Metrics**:
- Peer-review status (confirmed yes/no)
- Journal impact factor tier (Very High/High/Medium/Low)
- Institution reputation
- Citation count estimate (where available)

### 7.2 Key Findings Extraction

For each source, extracted:
- Primary research question or purpose
- Main findings relevant to PE education
- Safety considerations (where applicable)
- Practical applications for users
- Limitations or caveats

## 9. Limitations and Considerations

### 9.1 Access Limitations

**Paywall Constraints**:
- Some high-quality sources behind paywalls
- Relied on abstracts and summaries where full text unavailable
- Prioritized open access when multiple sources available on same topic

**Mitigation**:
- Selected 69% open access sources
- Used authoritative institutional summaries as alternatives
- Ensured sufficient coverage across all topics

### 9.2 Research Scope

**PE-Specific Research**:
- Limited peer-reviewed research specifically on PE for size
- Relied on related fields: urology, tissue engineering, sports medicine
- Applied principles from adjacent research areas

**Mitigation**:
- Used traction therapy research (medically established for Peyronie's)
- Applied tissue biology and biomechanics principles
- Included exercise science and recovery research

### 9.3 Publication Bias

**Awareness**:
- Positive results more likely to be published
- Safety concerns may be underreported
- Industry-funded research may have bias

**Mitigation**:
- Prioritized systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- Included clinical guidelines emphasizing safety
- Noted author affiliations and potential conflicts
