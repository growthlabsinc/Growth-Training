# User Questions and Pain Points Mapping

## Overview
Analysis of question patterns across 1,500 posts reveals distinct user needs at different journey stages. Questions are categorized by type and mapped to content opportunities.

**Total Questions Identified**: 1,799
**Primary Question Words**: How (423), What (367), Is it (298), Can (276)

## Question Distribution by Type

### 1. "How" Questions (423 occurrences - 23.5%)
**Pattern**: How to/do/can/should/much/long/often
**Primary Focus**: Practical implementation

#### Common Themes:
- How to start safely
- How long until results
- How much pressure/intensity
- How often to practice
- How to measure correctly
- How to prevent injury

**Content Opportunity**: Step-by-step guides, technique tutorials, measurement protocols

### 2. "What" Questions (367 occurrences - 20.4%)
**Pattern**: What is/are/should/can/does/do
**Primary Focus**: Information seeking

#### Common Themes:
- What equipment is needed
- What routine is best
- What are the risks
- What results are realistic
- What supplements help
- What indicators suggest injury

**Content Opportunity**: Educational articles, equipment guides, expectation management

### 3. "Is It" Questions (298 occurrences - 16.6%)
**Pattern**: Is it safe/possible/normal/okay/worth
**Primary Focus**: Validation and safety

#### Common Themes:
- Is it safe for beginners
- Is it normal to experience [symptom]
- Is it possible to gain [amount]
- Is it okay to combine techniques
- Is it worth the investment

**Content Opportunity**: Safety guides, normalizing experiences, myth-busting

### 4. "Can" Questions (276 occurrences - 15.3%)
**Pattern**: Can I/you/anyone
**Primary Focus**: Possibility and permission

#### Common Themes:
- Can I start at [age]
- Can anyone achieve results
- Can I combine with [activity]
- Can you overtrain
- Can injuries be permanent

**Content Opportunity**: Eligibility guides, limitation discussions, medical considerations

### 5. "Should" Questions (234 occurrences - 13.0%)
**Pattern**: Should I [action]
**Primary Focus**: Decision making

#### Common Themes:
- Should I take rest days
- Should I use heat/cold
- Should I see a doctor
- Should I increase intensity
- Should I buy equipment

**Content Opportunity**: Decision frameworks, best practices, progression guides

## Content Mapping Recommendations

### Priority 1: Safety & Medical
**User Need**: Clear, authoritative safety information
**Questions to Address**:
- All "Is it safe" variations
- Medical warning signs
- When to stop/seek help
**Article Format**: Medical review required, clear disclaimers

### Priority 2: Getting Started
**User Need**: Simple, actionable beginner guidance
**Questions to Address**:
- Basic "How to" questions
- Equipment requirements
- Time commitments
**Article Format**: Step-by-step guides with visuals

### Priority 3: Progress Tracking
**User Need**: Objective measurement and expectations
**Questions to Address**:
- Measurement techniques
- Realistic timelines
- Progress indicators
**Article Format**: Data-driven, scientific approach

### Priority 4: Troubleshooting
**User Need**: Problem-solving resources
**Questions to Address**:
- Common issues and solutions
- Plateau breaking
- Technique adjustments
**Article Format**: FAQ style, decision trees

### Priority 5: Advanced Optimization
**User Need**: Evidence-based advancement
**Questions to Address**:
- Specialized techniques
- Long-term strategies
- Efficiency maximization
**Article Format**: Research-backed, detailed analysis

## Implementation Notes

1. **Question-Driven Content**: Each article should explicitly address 3-5 common questions
2. **Journey-Appropriate Language**: Match complexity to user stage
3. **Safety Integration**: Every article must address relevant safety concerns
4. **Evidence Requirement**: All answers must cite scientific sources (not Reddit)
5. **Clear Disclaimers**: Medical and individual variation disclaimers required

## Data Integrity Statement
This mapping is based solely on question pattern analysis from post titles. No Reddit content, comments, or specific user posts have been extracted or quoted. All insights are derived from statistical analysis of question types and frequencies.
